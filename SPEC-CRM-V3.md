_Planned 2026-09-08 after a 3-round interview, a repo/data audit, competitor research (`AI_Training/research/crm-teardown-2026-09-08.md`) and an Opus design review. Approved by Anthony 2026-09-08. This file is the spec of record; builders read it from their worktree._

## Context

The CRM lies to Anthony in two places and can't answer his money question at all.

- **"Waiting on a yes" lists finished jobs.** A quote only leaves `pending` when the customer taps Accept on `/q`. Every other way a job moves forward (schedule card, crew Done, mark-paid, Stripe webhook, status dropdown) leaves the quote row untouched. Verified in prod: Diane Cervantes, Nancy Nesbitt, Joan Leatherwood are `complete` jobs with `pending` quotes; 14 of the 40 pending/help quotes sit on jobs already scheduled or complete.
- **"Needs scheduling" lists finished jobs.** The 8 rows (Weber $750, Morgon $500, Gallo $299, Shawn $200, Rash $150, Craigle $200, Antonio $299, Daisy $0) really are `approved` with no date and invoices still `sent`. He did the work without walking the three-step ceremony (Cal.com booking, mark complete, mark paid), so the CRM never learned.
- **Revenue is three numbers.** Dashboard tile = jobs marked complete, paid or not ($13,150). Invoices page = paid invoices, tips excluded ($6,383). Follow-ups page = every job's price at any status. Tips ($240) are in no total. 33 of 38 complete jobs have no `completed_at`.
- **Clients is a flat table.** No last-service-by-service, no "due", no offers, no sort on the phone (the surface he actually uses). `service_type` is free text with 23 spellings.

Same root cause everywhere: the CRM only knows what happened if Anthony tells it in several places, and nothing derives state from the strongest available signal (money).

**Outcome:** a CRM that closes a job in one tap, derives every list from real state, shows paid money as revenue with unpaid work flagged, and turns 69 clients into a ranked list of who to contact and what to offer.

## Facts that shape the design (verified against prod today)

- `squeegee_invoices.client_id` is **NULL on every auto-generated invoice** (`createInvoiceForQuote` never sets it). Per-client money must resolve through `job_id`.
- `sms_messages.related_id` is **uuid**, so compound dedupe keys (client + service) go in `kind` (`rebook_house_wash`), never in `related_id`.
- Invoice numbers are `count(*) + 1001` in three places (`invoices/route.ts:35`, `quotes/[token]/respond/route.ts:59`, `parser.ts:234`): collide under concurrency and **repeat after a delete**.
- `mark-paid/route.ts` has no idempotency guard; the webhook's `if (status === 'paid') return` is a read-then-write race. A double tap sends two receipts and two review asks.
- `appointment_date` is a `date`; `new Date("2026-09-02")` is midnight **UTC** = 8pm ET the day before. All date-only values are anchored at **noon ET** before they become instants.
- PostgREST returns `numeric` as **strings**; coerce with `Number()` at one boundary.
- `sendSms` returns `sent: true` in test mode (redirected to `SMS_TEST_TO`). Anything that counts touches must check `!result.test`.
- Job DELETE cascades invoices (paid ones included) and quotes: one delete silently changes a month's revenue.
- Another Claude session pushed to this repo today. Fetch + rebase before every integration.

## Architecture

### One snapshot, pure math (no SQL view)
`lib/squeegee/metrics.ts` loads clients + jobs + quotes + invoices + plans + expenses once (service-role, ~300 rows) and every number comes from pure functions over that snapshot: `computeClientMetrics`, `computeRevenue`, `derivePipelineState`, `computeOffers`. No Postgres view: a view defaults to `SELECT` for `anon` and doesn't inherit base-table RLS (a real leak surface), needs the service mapping duplicated in SQL, and buys nothing at this size. One code path serves the Dashboard, Revenue, Clients, both crons and the Monday digest, so they can never disagree.

### Canonical services, computed on read
`lib/squeegee/services.ts` (import-free so scripts can load it): `Service = house_wash | windows | driveway | surfaces | pavers | pool_deck | gutters | roof | other`. `normalizeService(raw)` returns the **earliest-positioned** match in a regex pattern table (`pool deck` before `deck`; "Roof + Gutter Blow Off" → roof; "Front Entryway Softwash" → surfaces); `normalizeServices(raw)` splits on `, / & + and`, maps, dedupes; placeholders (`Pending Quote`, `TBD`, `Service`) → `[]`, non-empty unknowns → `["other"]`. `deriveJobServices(quoteLines, service_type)` prefers quote line names. No stored column: nothing to drift, and a complete job whose services resolve to `[]` shows up as a Data-health row. Judgement calls documented in the file header: Back Deck / Retaining Wall / Entryway → surfaces.

### Job state derived, not declared
`derivePipelineState(job, quotes, invoices)` → `needs_quote | waiting_on_yes | needs_scheduling | scheduled | awaiting_closeout | paid | cancelled | excluded`, strongest signal first: paid invoice > `completed_at` > appointment > quote status. Every list filters on this (Jobber/Housecall pattern: boards query open work only).

### Money settles in one place, with a latch
`lib/squeegee/payments.ts` → `markInvoicePaid(sb, invoiceId, {method, paidAt, tip, amountOverride, card, stripePaymentIntentId, skipReceipt, skipReview})`. The update is a compare-and-set: `.update({status:'paid', …}).eq('id', id).neq('status','paid').select().maybeSingle()`; only the row that flipped runs job-close, quote settlement, activity, receipt, review ask, Slack. Today's `mark-paid/route.ts` and the Stripe webhook become callers; close-out is the third. Also fixes both existing double-send races.

### Automations through the one send path
Everything customer-facing goes through `lib/squeegee/sms.ts` (opt-out always blocks, consent per decision 7, every send logged, SMS_LIVE gate). Dedupe follows `smsReviewOnce` (lookup in `sms_messages` by kind + related). Toggles and caps live in `squeegee_settings` so he flips them from his phone.
